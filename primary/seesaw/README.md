# Balancing a Seesaw

Primary science: seesaw examples and the load/fulcrum relationship. Move two riders and change their masses to discover that a heavier rider can balance a lighter rider by sitting nearer the fulcrum. Open `index.html` directly; everything works offline.

## Figure plan

The beam rocks about a fixed central fulcrum; the rider with the larger mass × distance has the downward turning effect. A colorful playground apparatus uses an oblique camera (85° azimuth, 20° elevation), shaded solid faces and a back-to-front face order.

Purposeful parts: four rubber feet, foundation plate, inset safety mat, four mat anchor plates and four fasteners, two fulcrum foot plates, two triangular cheeks, two cheek caps, axle block, two axle covers, two axle fasteners, beam, two end caps, two underside reinforcing strips, distance strip and rooted ticks, two sliding seat shoes, two seats, four seat bolts, four handle uprights and two grips. Both riders face inward, with mirrored noses, eyes, arms, handles and seated legs. Seats are mounted on top of the beam; far legs are drawn behind it and near legs in front. These physical drawing slots prevent a long beam face from covering a seat. Each rider has two shoes, two shins, two thighs, hips, torso, collar, two arms, head, hair cap, nose, visible eye and ear. More than 70 solid components; no pipes, cables or curved-body dependencies. The geometry is generated by portable `app.js` using the repository projection helpers. Color identifies the coral left rider and blue right rider.

## Controls

Each rider has a 40–100 kg mass slider (1 kg steps) and a 0.5–2.5 m distance slider (0.01 m steps). Distance is measured along the beam from the fixed fulcrum. Drag either rider or its fullscreen movement label; click a rider to select it. Left/Right or Up/Down move the selected rider outward/inward by 0.1 m (Shift: 0.5 m); Home/End select distance limits. The select buttons provide keyboard access to both riders. Mass remains adjustable through the normal-page sliders.

Balance riders preserves the left rider's turning effect when possible and moves the right rider to match it. If that distance would exceed the limits, both positions change to the nearest feasible common mass-distance product. The mathematical position can fall between slider steps; values display three decimals and balance uses the exact value. Moving a slider afterward uses its stated step. Auto explore uses a 60 kg left rider and a 40 kg right rider and varies both positions; Pause stops it. Reduced motion gives a single static comparison. Reset restores left 60 kg and right 40 kg at 1.5 m, left selection, graph and paused auto mode. Animation and auto exploration pause while hidden or offscreen.

Fullscreen retains the apparatus, two independently operable movement labels and current output. Shared fullscreen handles Escape, native/fallback mode and focus restoration. The fulcrum is fixed and has no movement label.

## Model and limits

Gravity is 9.8 m/s². Horizontal-beam reference turning effects are τL = mL g dL and τR = mR g dR, in N·m. At beam angle θ the actual gravitational moments are these values multiplied by cos θ. The common factor cancels, so balance requires mL dL = mR dR at every allowed angle. The graph and comparison bars show horizontal reference moments, keeping distance comparisons clear. The fulcrum supports the beam and defines the point about which the loads turn it; its own force has zero lever arm.

The beam and seats are treated as massless; rider masses act at seat centers. Friction, rider body movement and inertia are excluded. **Tilt is an illustrative sign-and-strength indicator, not a predicted free-seesaw equilibrium:** target θ = 12° tanh[(mL dL − mR dR)/30 kg·m], with exponential following (0.2 s). Positive θ lowers the left side. A real unbalanced frictionless seesaw continues toward its stop; a balanced one need not return to horizontal. Here equal moments return the drawing to level to make balance readable. Balance tolerance is 10⁻⁷ kg·m. No arbitrary percentage band labels unequal loads as balanced.
